Table card

Your table works through three steps in about fifty minutes. A rapporteur writes; everyone decides. Use the printed maps on the table and these sheets. Nothing here is a validated result: it is a working draft produced in a workshop.

Open the tool on one laptop or tablet per table:  or open index.html from the USB stick on your table. It works without internet. Work in the 2D map; the 3D view is for the projector.
1
Weight the suitability criteria (12 minutes). Rate them as they matter in your own context, not in Colombia. In the tool: section 1. On paper: Sheet 1. First choose the most important criterion, then rate the others against it; then choose the least important and rate the others against it. The scale runs from 2 to 9; the value 1 is reserved for the Best and the Worst themselves.
2
Place conflict and governance (16 minutes). In the tool: section 2. On paper: Sheet 2 and the maps. GENTE kept the two indices outside the score, as an interpretive layer. Decide as a table where they should go: interpretive layer, weighted criteria, veto threshold, or sequencing tiers. Apply your choice to the table of departments by hand and see who moves, who leaves, and who stays.
3
Record and export (9 minutes). In the tool: section 3, then Download HTML. On paper: Sheet 3. Five short answers, one per section of a policy brief outline. This sheet is what your table hands in.

The question under everything

The conflict index measures armed and humanitarian conflict. In La Guajira, wind projects are shaped by consultation and territorial disputes that the index does not measure. So La Guajira leads the wind ranking with a low conflict score. That is not an error of the data; it is a question about what the indicator measures and for whom. What would your own indicator miss?

Reading the maps

Green is always the desirable end of the scale: high viability, or low conflict, or high governance. Grey departments have no data for that technology. Hatched departments have been excluded by a threshold. On the two index maps (NCI, NGI) the legend direction is reversed on purpose so that green stays desirable.

Rapporteur: type the table name in section 3. Export before closing the page: nothing is sent anywhere, so an unexported record is lost. On paper, write the table name on every sheet and hand in Sheet 3.

Weights by hand (approximation)

Without the tool, use the consistent-case approximation: each weight is proportional to 1 / aBj. Fill the column 1 / aBj (the Best's row is 1), add the column, and divide each value by the total. Write the result next to the expert weight and note where your table diverges most.

Consistency check by hand

For each criterion j other than Best and Worst, compute CRj = | aBj × ajW − aBW | ÷ ( aBW² − aBW ). The table is consistent if the largest CRj is below the threshold for your aBW:

The five sections below follow the structure of the GENTE policy brief. One or two sentences each is enough.

1 · Problem

State the siting decision you face in your own context, in one sentence.

2 · Strategic finding

What did the ranking and the map reveal that a technical assessment alone would not?

3 · Critical risk

What does the conflict or governance indicator not capture in your context?

4 · Policy implication

Where would you place conflict and governance in the model, and who should decide that?

5 · Priority recommendation

One action, one responsible actor, one time horizon.
